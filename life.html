From Suvendu Adhikari to Chief Minister Sri Suvendu Adhikari

Before Nabanna, before Nandigram, before the Assembly — a childhood in Purba Medinipur, a political family, and a slow climb through the ranks. This is the fuller life behind the six-year headline.

Photo via Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)

Born into one of Purba Medinipur's most prominent political families, in a district he would later carry to a national result.

Suvendu Adhikari was born on 15 December 1970 in Karkuli, a village in Purba Medinipur, West Bengal. He is the eldest son of Sisir Kumar Adhikari, a veteran parliamentarian, and grew up in a household where public life was the family business rather than a distant ambition.

He is reported to hold a master's degree — some accounts cite Netaji Subhas Open University, others Rabindra Bharati University; the site has not been able to confirm which is correct, and this should be verified against his official biodata before publishing. What's better documented is the shape of his early years: less time in front of cameras, more time in the district's villages and party offices, learning the ground game he would later be known for.

He began in the Indian National Congress before moving to the Trinamool Congress, where he rose steadily through the ranks over the following two decades — long before the December 2020 break that turned him into a national political story.

Before 2020

Two decades inside the TMC

The years that don't usually make the highlight reel — but that built the organiser who later rebuilt the BJP's Bengal unit from nothing.

2007

The Nandigram movement

As a TMC leader, Adhikari led local resistance to a proposed land acquisition for a chemical hub in Nandigram — the episode that first brought him statewide attention and permanently tied his name to the constituency.

2009–19

Two terms as MP, Tamluk

Elected to the Lok Sabha from Tamluk, the seat his brother Dibyendu would later hold, giving the family a continuous parliamentary presence from Purba Medinipur.

2016–20

State cabinet minister

Served as a minister in the West Bengal state government under the TMC, holding portfolios including transport and irrigation, before the December 2020 break with the party.

Since 9 May 2026

From karyakarta to Chief Minister

The title changed on 9 May 2026, but the working style — district visits, direct meetings, a hands-on approach to the bureaucracy — carried straight from the opposition bench into Nabanna.

9 May

Sworn in as the 9th Chief Minister of West Bengal

Took the oath of office at Brigade Parade Ground, Kolkata — the first BJP Chief Minister in the state's history.

1 June

Cabinet expanded

Brought 35 more MLAs into government in the first major reshuffle of the new ministry.

2 June

Meets the state's newest IPS officers

Addressed the 2023-batch IPS probationers at Nabanna, an early signal of hands-on engagement with the administration he now leads.
