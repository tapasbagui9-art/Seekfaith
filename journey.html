Six assembly years. One road to Nabanna.

No single wave carried this campaign — it was built booth by booth, district by district, across six years on the opposition bench. Here is that road, year by year.

2020
Departure

Leaves the TMC, joins the BJP

In December 2020, Adhikari ended more than two decades with the Trinamool Congress and joined the BJP ahead of the 2021 assembly election — a move widely covered as one of the most consequential defections in the state's recent politics, and the first step of the six-year road to come.

2021
Nandigram

Wins Nandigram, becomes Leader of the Opposition

Contesting his home constituency on a BJP ticket, he defeated Chief Minister Mamata Banerjee in the closely watched Nandigram contest. Sworn in as an MLA in May 2021, he was named Leader of the Opposition in the West Bengal Assembly the same month — the BJP's most prominent face in the state, despite falling short of forming the government that year.

2021–24
Organisation

Booth by booth, district by district

The years on the opposition bench were spent largely outside Kolkata: a relentless travel schedule — by various party accounts, several hundred kilometres on many days — carried him from booth committees to district units across all 23 districts, rebuilding a cadre base and keeping in direct contact with karyakartas rather than governing from the Assembly floor.

2024
Setback

Kanthi Lok Sabha

Adhikari contested the Kanthi Lok Sabha seat on a BJP ticket in 2024 and did not win it — a reminder that the six years were not a straight line. The organisation he'd built kept working through the loss, and fed straight into the following campaign.

2025
Consolidation

Sharpening the opposition machine

As Leader of the Opposition, he continued to press the case against the TMC government on law and order and on the treatment of BJP workers, while turning the party's district committees toward the 2026 campaign in earnest.

2026
Victory

A historic mandate

Contesting both Nandigram and Bhabanipur in the 2026 assembly election, Adhikari won both — defeating Mamata Banerjee a second time, this time in her own Bhabanipur seat, by 73,917 votes. The BJP secured a landslide majority of around 206–208 of 294 seats, ending the TMC's fifteen-year hold on the state.

9 May 2026
Nabanna

Sworn in as the 9th Chief Minister of West Bengal

Elected leader of the BJP legislature party on 8 May, Adhikari took the oath of office the next day at Brigade Parade Ground in Kolkata — fittingly, on Rabindranath Tagore's birth anniversary — the first Chief Minister of West Bengal from the BJP, in a ceremony attended by the party's national leadership.

Organisation

Booth to Bhavan

The climb that the campaign was actually built on — not a single wave, but a structure rebuilt one level at a time.

01

Booth committees

The base unit — a booth president and a small team responsible for every voter list in that booth.

02

Mandal & block units

Booths grouped into blocks, coordinating logistics, local grievances, and turnout across a cluster of villages or wards.

03

District committees

All 23 districts brought under a direct reporting line to the state leadership, visited in person on a recurring cycle.

04

State leadership

Leader of the Opposition, coordinating strategy, candidate selection, and messaging across the state.

05

Nabanna

The state secretariat — the seat of the Chief Minister, and the structure's final step.

On the opposition bench

Standing with the karyakartas

A central plank of Adhikari's six years in opposition was his role as the party's most visible defender of its own workers.

01

A vocal defence of party workers

Throughout his tenure as Leader of the Opposition, Adhikari and other BJP leaders repeatedly alleged that party workers were being targeted with politically motivated cases by the state government, and he positioned himself as the leader who showed up — in courts, in districts, and at protests — on their behalf.

02

Presence over distance

Supporters point to his travel schedule as the practical form this took: district visits, local unit meetings, and direct contact with workers and families involved in disputed cases, rather than managing the opposition solely from Kolkata.

These are characterisations made by Adhikari and the BJP during a period of sharp political contest with the TMC government; they reflect a contested political narrative rather than settled fact, and are presented here as part of the campaign's own account of these years.
